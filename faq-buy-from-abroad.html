Do I need to be in Japan to buy a house?

Short answer: no. The purchase itself can be done entirely from abroad through a judicial scrivener. What people underestimate is not the buying - it's the three things that need a person in Japan after you own it.

How buying from abroad works

There is no citizenship or visa requirement to own property. A judicial scrivener (司法書士, shihoshoshi) handles the registration; you sign a power of attorney. Where a Japanese buyer uses a registered seal certificate, a non-resident substitutes a signature certificate - typically issued by your local notary or your country's embassy. Financing is the hard part: Japanese banks rarely lend to non-residents, so most overseas buyers of cheap houses pay cash.

The three things that do need a person in Japan

  • A tax agent (納税管理人, nozei kanrinin). Property tax notices arrive as paper, in Japanese, to a Japanese address - the city will not mail them abroad. A non-resident owner appoints someone in Japan (anyone: a friend, a firm) to receive the notices and pay. Skip this and the bill you never saw quietly becomes arrears.
  • Eyes on the house. Grass, snow, leaks, and the neighbour's opinion of your jungle do not pause between your visits. Budget for upkeep and for someone who checks.
  • Paperwork after the purchase. Non-residents must file a report on the acquisition under the foreign exchange law (a formality, but a required one - your scrivener can point you to it).

The exit surprise: selling as a non-resident

When a non-resident sells Japanese real estate, the buyer is generally required to withhold 10.21% of the sale price and pay it to the tax office. You reclaim the difference by filing a tax return - but at settlement, the cash that arrives is smaller than the price you agreed. The exception: no withholding when the buyer is an individual purchasing it for their own or family residence at ¥100,000,000 or less. Ask which case you are in before you agree a price.

What to actually do

  • Line up the judicial scrivener and ask about the signature certificate early - embassy appointments can be the slowest step.
  • Decide who your tax agent will be before completion, and file the appointment.
  • Put the yearly line - tax, upkeep, someone checking - into the budget next to the purchase price. On cheap houses, the decade of ownership usually costs more than the house.
