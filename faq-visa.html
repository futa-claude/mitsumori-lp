Does buying a house in Japan give you residency?

Short answer: no. Owning property in Japan gives you no visa, no residency points, no path to citizenship - nothing. Anyone can buy; buying changes nothing about how long you may stay. And the famous workaround got six times harder in October 2025.

What owning actually gets you

Japan has no restrictions on foreign ownership - you can own land and building outright with no visa at all. But your stay is governed entirely by your immigration status. For most Western passport holders that means visa-free visits of up to 90 days at a time. In practice, non-resident owners live with their house in visits, not in residence.

The workaround that just closed

For years the popular plan was: buy an akiya, run it as a guesthouse or small business, and apply for a Business Manager visa - which used to require about ¥5,000,000 in capital.

That changed on October 16, 2025. The Business Manager visa now requires:

  • ¥30,000,000 or more in capital - six times the old bar;
  • employing at least one full-time local employee (a Japanese national, permanent resident, or similar status);
  • Japanese ability at roughly B2 level - from you or that employee.

People who already hold the visa have a transition period: they must meet the new standards by October 2028 to keep renewing.

Other routes exist - work visas, spouse visas, a digital nomad visa with its own high income bar - but none of them are created or helped by owning a house.

What to actually do

  • Plan the visa before you plan the house. Decide how you will legally spend time in Japan first; let the house fit that answer.
  • If your plan involves any business-based status, talk to an immigration specialist (行政書士, gyoseishoshi) before you buy - not after.
  • Budget for absence: a house you can only visit needs someone to check it, and the costs run whether you are there or not.
