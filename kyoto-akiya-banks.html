What an akiya bank actually is

An akiya bank (空き家バンク) is a listing service run by the town hall, not a real-estate company. Owners of vacant houses register them; the municipality publishes them — often at prices, and in villages, that never reach the commercial portals. Three things to know before you click:

  • Most banks require buyer registration first. You file a short application (sometimes with a statement that you intend to actually live there) before you can view details or visit. Confirmed required in Fukuchiyama, Ayabe, Kameoka, Kasagi, Wazuka, Ine and Minamiyamashiro; Joyo is the one town that says none is needed.
  • The town does not negotiate for you. Deals go through a local licensed agent, or directly between you and the owner.
  • Everything is in Japanese. Your browser's translate button handles most of it; application forms are where you'll want help.

Northern Kyoto — where the listings actually are

The pattern is stark: the further from Kyoto City, the more houses. The Tango and Chutan regions (2–3 hours north of the city, on and near the Sea of Japan) hold most of the prefecture's supply.

How to actually use one

  • 1. Pick the town before the house. Supply is in the north; tax rates differ (1.4%–1.7%); winters differ more. The listing is the last step, not the first.
  • 2. Register as a buyer. Most banks want a one-page application first — some ask about your intention to settle. This filters out browsers, which is exactly why the good cheap houses are here and not on commercial portals.
  • 3. Ask the town's counter. Every bank has a human window (移住 or 定住 section). Small towns will genuinely walk you around — Kasagi even assigns a relocation advisor to guide viewings.
  • 4. Expect Japanese, everywhere. Forms, calls, viewings. Budget for help, or bring a friend who reads Japanese.

The honest part

Akiya banks are the real, unpolished inventory — and that cuts both ways. Some listings sit for years for good reasons: no rebuild rights, no parking, structural issues nobody photographed. The bank page will not tell you that. Check the road, the tax notice, and the running costs before you fall in love — that is what the rest of this site is for.
